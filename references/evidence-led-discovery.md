# Evidence-Led Product Discovery

Use this reference when the important unknown is whether a problem is real, who feels it most, which workaround competes with the product, whether anyone changes behavior or pays, or whether one need should outrank another. It strengthens the first DZ decision; it is not a separate BRD or MRD stage and does not force research onto a clear personal utility.

## Keep three kinds of truth separate

Never use one kind as a substitute for another:

1. **Problem evidence** shows what people actually experience or do today.
2. **Owner decisions** set the product's chosen audience, investment, limits, and test thresholds.
3. **Outcome evidence** shows what happened when the proposed product or manual substitute was tried.

A public complaint can support “this frustration exists.” It does not by itself prove market size, willingness to pay, priority, or a numerical success target. A target such as “five people complete the flow” may be a deliberately chosen cheap test; label it as an owner-selected threshold rather than pretending a user quote established it.

## Start from the decision, not from a search quota

Before collecting material, write the one question whose answer could change what DZ recommends. Examples include:

- Do independent recruiters repeatedly lose time cleaning interview notes?
- Do they already pay, improvise, or accept the problem?
- Is accurate evidence linking more important than polished prose?

Then collect only enough material to decide the next cheap step. Do not require a fixed number of posts, sources, interviews, or search calls. A personal tool may rely on the owner's repeated observed behavior. A larger or public product normally needs broader evidence from the people expected to adopt it.

When public research is useful, search across different source types rather than repeating one platform. Keep the query free of secrets, customer text, private code, internal URLs, and unpublished strategy. Do not scrape or store personal information merely to make the evidence set look larger.

## Evidence strength and limitations

Assess the claim, not the prestige of the source. Prefer the closest evidence available:

1. observed behavior, repeated workaround, purchase, cancellation, or completed task;
2. first-party interviews, support conversations, usage records, or owned research with a known sample;
3. public first-person comments or reviews from a plausibly relevant user;
4. current product documentation, pricing, credible research, or market reporting;
5. an analyst or AI inference.

This is an ordering guide, not an automatic score. For every important item, inspect:

- **directness:** did it observe behavior or only stated interest?
- **relevance:** does the person, situation, market, and product match this decision?
- **recency:** could the behavior, price, competitor, or rule have changed?
- **traceability:** can another reviewer reach the original material and date?
- **sample limits:** who is missing, overrepresented, self-selected, or duplicated?
- **contradiction:** what credible evidence points the other way?

Discussion volume is not market size. Emotional wording is not automatically severity. “I would pay” is not payment. Search snippets and copied summaries are leads until the original source is checked.

## Durable evidence card

When a writable project exists and the evidence materially affects Intent, keep the cards in `docs/sdlc/discovery-evidence.md`. One card may support several claims; do not duplicate it in multiple documents.

```markdown
## Evidence {ID}
- Decision question:
- Observed material: exact behavior, result, short excerpt, or measurement
- Source kind: observed behavior / first-party material / public user material / documentation or research / inference
- Source: person or dataset when appropriate, URL or file, author/publisher, and observed or published date
- Supports:
- Does not prove:
- Relevance, recency, and sample limits:
- Contradictory evidence:
- Privacy or usage restriction:
- Confidence: direct / useful but limited / weak lead
- Checked by and date:
```

Do not copy sensitive raw material into the card. Use a permitted reference, redacted excerpt, aggregate, or file location. Preserve a user's own wording when it matters, but keep quotations short and accurate.

## From evidence to a product recommendation

Before asking for acceptance of the first decision, DZ must give one professionally owned recommendation:

- **Proceed:** enough evidence supports the problem and a small next investment.
- **Proceed if:** one named uncertainty must pass a cheap test first or remain an explicit condition.
- **Hold or rethink:** current evidence contradicts the idea, the reachable user is unclear, the workaround is already good enough, or the likely value does not justify the next investment.

Also name the strongest reason, the strongest contrary signal, the cheapest decisive test, and what result would cause DZ to narrow, stop, or revisit the idea. The user may still choose to continue when they own that decision; record the difference between their choice and the evidence-based recommendation instead of relabeling the evidence.

In beginner-facing Chinese, say “值得继续,” “先确认这一件事再继续,” or “现在先别投入更多时间,” then explain the concrete consequence. Do not teach BRD, MRD, confidence scoring, or research vocabulary unless the user asks.

## Research self-check

Before treating research as decision-ready, check:

- every material claim points to a real card or is labeled an owner decision or inference;
- the original source, date, and relevant context were checked when available;
- duplicates and reposts do not inflate frequency;
- quotations keep their meaning and are not search-snippet inventions;
- stated interest, actual behavior, and payment remain separate;
- numerical market or success claims have an appropriate source or are clearly chosen test thresholds;
- missing groups, inaccessible platforms, recency limits, and conflicting evidence remain visible;
- the conclusion answers the original decision question instead of rewarding the most dramatic material.

If the evidence is too weak, do not manufacture certainty. Recommend the smallest interview, manual service, landing-page test, prototype observation, or other reversible check that could change the decision.
